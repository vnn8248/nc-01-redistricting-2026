How this was built

  • District lines are the legislature's official plans: S.L. 2023-145, used in 2024, and S.L. 2025-95, used in 2026. Both are whole counties plus a few precincts. Both plans draw the same line through five Granville precincts, and the 2026 plan also takes part of one Onslow precinct, Swansboro. The lines split Salem, in Granville, and Swansboro; precinct results can't be divided, so all their votes are counted even though about 45% of Salem's voters and 35% of Swansboro's residents are in other districts.
  • The rebuilt totals add every 2024 vote in each district's counties, including early, absentee and provisional ballots, plus the Granville and Swansboro precincts. Rebuilding the 2024 House race on the old lines reproduces its official total exactly, and the precincts match the NC-1 ballot in NCSBE's 2026 results file.
  • The county view counts every vote. The precinct view can only shade votes reported by precinct. Those counties are hatched in the precinct view. Their precinct colors mostly show election-day voters, who leaned more Republican than early voters.
  • The race maps show citizens 18 and older, the people who can vote, from the Census Bureau's 2020-2024 American Community Survey estimates. Those are published for block groups; each is split among its census blocks in proportion to the 2020 census count of adults in each group. The total for the 2024 district matches the Census Bureau's own figure for it within 0.1%. "White" and "Black" are non-Hispanic and one race alone; "other" is Hispanic residents of any race plus every other group. Each dot is {{people_per_dot}} voting-age citizens of one group, placed at random inside their census block. The 2026 district also takes in part of Swansboro, in Onslow County.
  • Margins are the Democrat's share minus the Republican's share of all votes cast in the race, so third-party votes count in the total.
  • The "Davis on new lines" estimate assumes he runs the same {{davis_edge}} points ahead of the top of the ticket. It's a yardstick, not a forecast.
