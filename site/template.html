U.S. House · North Carolina District 1 · Nov 3, 2026

NC-1, before and after the redraw

Don Davis won the 1st District by 1.7 points in 2024. In October 2025 the legislature traded four of its inland counties for six coastal ones, and the same 2024 voters would have given Trump an 11-point win inside the new lines instead of 3.

The same 2024 votes, counted inside each version

Every 2024 statewide race moves 6 to 8 points toward Republicans on the new lines. Davis ran 4.7 points ahead of Harris in 2024. The same edge on the new lines would still lose by about 6.5.

The county swap

The four counties removed voted for Trump by 9 points combined. The six added voted for him by 29, and they cast more votes. The 18 counties kept in both versions split almost exactly even.

How this was built

  • District lines are the legislature's official plans: S.L. 2023-145, used in 2024, and S.L. 2025-95, used in 2026. Both are whole-county districts except for the same six Granville precincts.
  • The rebuilt totals add every 2024 vote in each district's counties, including early, absentee and provisional ballots, plus the six Granville precincts. Rebuilding the 2024 House race on the old lines reproduces its official total exactly.
  • The maps can only shade votes reported by precinct. Those counties are hatched. Their precinct colors mostly show election-day voters, who leaned more Republican than early voters.
  • Margins are the Democrat's share minus the Republican's share of all votes cast in the race, so third-party votes count in the total.
  • The "Davis on new lines" estimate assumes he runs the same 4.7 points ahead of the top of the ticket. It's a yardstick, not a forecast.
